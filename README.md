## Research

My research examines how cardiac electrical activity becomes unstable, from action potentials in individual cells to the propagation of waves through tissue. I combine detailed physiological simulations with simpler mathematical models that make the underlying mechanisms easier to analyze.

### Cardiac memory and nonlinear dynamics

The electrical response of a cardiac cell depends on its recent history. Slow recovery of ion channels and changes in intracellular ion concentrations can influence subsequent action potentials. My work investigates how this short-term memory contributes to alternans, oscillations, and chaos, particularly in models of diseased conditions such as early repolarization and reduced repolarization reserve.

Iterated maps are an important part of this work. They reduce complex, beat-to-beat behavior to a mathematical form that supports stability analysis and helps explain why a model transitions from a regular response to an unstable one.

### Ion channels, feedback, and arrhythmias

I have studied the interactions among membrane voltage, intracellular calcium and sodium, and potassium currents. These feedback relationships can change the stability of cardiac excitation. My publications examine the transient outward potassium current, small-conductance calcium-activated potassium channels, and the effects of intracellular ion accumulation.

### From single cells to cardiac tissue

At the tissue level, my work includes spiral-wave breakup, phase 2 reentry, and spatially discordant repolarization alternans. These studies connect cellular electrical dynamics with patterns of activity that can promote arrhythmias.

I have also investigated feedback pacing methods for controlling voltage-driven instabilities in computational models. Earlier work applied quantitative modeling to chronic myeloid leukemia, examining cell-population models in light of findings from radiobiology.

## Computational approach

My work draws on:

- **Dynamical systems:** stability, bifurcations, oscillations, and chaos.
- **Physiological modeling:** cardiac action potentials, ion-channel kinetics, and intracellular ion dynamics.
- **Numerical simulation:** exploring behavior in models of individual cells and cardiac tissue.
- **Model reduction:** using iterated maps to identify mechanisms within more detailed simulations.
- **Quantitative analysis:** connecting mathematical results with physiological questions.

### Doctoral dissertation

**[Cardiac Memory in the Genesis of Arrhythmias](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/49z2b34v)** — UCLA, 2019.

My dissertation examines how short-term cardiac memory contributes to action-potential instability, how that instability might be controlled in models, and how cellular dynamics can lead to arrhythmias at the tissue level.
